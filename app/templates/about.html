About CinePhile

CinePhile is a movie discovery app that suggests films using content-based machine learning. It was designed and developed by Ayush Sabale as a Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) lab and continuous-evaluation project.

How the recommendations work

  1. Data: {{ total }} films are stored in a PostgreSQL database (genres, themes, cast, director, plot, rating).
  2. Vectorise: each film becomes a text document. Genres, themes and directors are weighted higher than plot words, then converted to TF-IDF vectors with scikit-learn.
  3. Compare: cosine similarity measures how close two vectors are. The closest films are shown under “More like this”.
  4. Personalise: films in My List are averaged into a taste profile, and every other film is ranked against it.
  5. Re-rank: 90% content similarity is blended with 10% community rating so good films surface first.

HCI principles applied

  • Visibility of system status: toasts confirm actions, buttons show pressed state, search announces result counts.
  • Recognition over recall: poster cards, genre chips and breadcrumbs keep options visible.
  • User control and freedom: every addition to My List can be undone with the same button.
  • Explainability and trust: each suggestion shows its similarity score and the traits it shares.
  • Accessibility: keyboard navigation, skip link, ARIA labels, visible focus, high contrast and reduced-motion support.
  • Efficiency: press / anywhere to jump to search.

The full mapping to Nielsen's heuristics, Gestalt laws and Fitts's / Hick's laws is in docs/HCI_PRINCIPLES.md in the repository.
