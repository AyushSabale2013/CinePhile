About CinePhile

CinePhile is a movie discovery application that generates film recommendations using content-based machine learning and information retrieval ranking. It was designed and developed by Ayush Sabale as an HCI and machine learning project.

Recommendation Architecture & Feature Engineering

  1. Dataset & Schema: A curated catalogue of {{ total }} distinct, verified feature films from TMDB, containing validated release years, runtimes, ratings, genres, thematic keywords, cast, directors, overviews, and official artwork.
  2. Feature Weighting & Vectorization: Structured attributes are weighted by domain discriminative power: genres (×3) and director (×3) provide primary stylistic signals; keywords (×2) and cast (×2) provide thematic and star signals; plot synopsis (×1) is stemmed and normalized. Unigrams and bigrams are vectorized using scikit-learn's TfidfVectorizer.
  3. Generalization & Leakage Prevention: The vectorizer vocabulary and IDF weights are fit strictly on the training split only (78%). Unseen test films and dynamic queries are projected into the fitted vector space with zero data leakage.
  4. Similarity Computation: Pairwise cosine similarity is computed via L2-normalized dot products. Personal taste profiles in My List are aggregated via an exponential recency-decay centroid.
  5. Bayesian Rating Blend: Content similarity (88%) is blended with an IMDB Bayesian confidence rating (12%) to promote quality while preserving content relevance.

Evaluation Methodology & Metrics

Because CinePhile is a ranking/retrieval engine rather than a discrete classifier, performance is evaluated using standard Information Retrieval ranking metrics on the held-out test set:

  • Hit Rate @ K: Fraction of test queries that retrieve at least one relevant title in the top K.
  • Precision @ K: Fraction of the top K recommendations that meet multi-attribute relevance criteria.
  • Recall @ K: Fraction of all relevant catalogue items captured in the top K.
  • NDCG @ K: Normalised Discounted Cumulative Gain, penalizing relevant items that appear lower in the ranking.
  • Intra-List Diversity & Coverage: Measures pairwise cosine distance within recommendation rails and catalogue reach.

Model Limitations & Similarity vs. Accuracy

Content-based systems compute vector alignment rather than classification labels. Similarity scores reflect textual and categorical affinity, not human ground-truth preference ratings. Pure metadata splits evaluate representation generalization, while true recommendation satisfaction requires logged user interaction signals.

HCI Principles Applied

  • Visibility of system status: Feedback toasts confirm watchlist updates with undo capability; loading states are explicitly communicated.
  • Recognition over recall: Visual poster cards, genre chips, and breadcrumbs keep options scannable.
  • Explainability and trust: Recommendations disclose their match percentage and shared attributes (director, genre, cast, keywords).
  • Accessibility: Semantic HTML, ARIA attributes, keyboard navigation, and high contrast ratios.
  • Efficiency: Press / anywhere to focus discovery search.
